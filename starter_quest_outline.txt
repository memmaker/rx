The starter/example quest that will set the tone for the game

Delivered to your home_terminal is a job offer from a new client:

"Extract $500 from the Ripperdoc in the northside residential area. He owes Jacob."

The offer is signed with a simple "Jacob".

The money has to be deposited in the dead_drop you always use.

If done correctly, the client will retrieve the money and leave a reward for the player.

Now the player should have these options:

- Get the money from the Ripperdoc
  - by stealing from his secret stash - POSSIBLE
  - by force - POSSIBLE
    - non-lethal - POSSIBLE
    - lethal - POSSIBLE
  - by persuasion - POSSIBLE
- Negotiate with the Ripperdoc
  - to pay you double - POSSIBLE
  - to pay you at least half - POSSIBLE
  - to reward you for going after the client - POSSIBLE
- Find the old ripperdoc, locked away in the cellar of the hospital
  - to kill him - POSSIBLE
  - to free him - POSSIBLE
  - to get information from him about the secret stash - POSSIBLE
- Pay out of his own pocket, or with money stolen from someone else - POSSIBLE
- Ignore the job - POSSIBLE
- Find out more about the client and go after him (from either the doc or grim)
  - to kill him - POSSIBLE
  - to get more payment
  - to intimidate him into leaving the Doc alone
  - to steal money from him

So how does the world react to the player's actions?

Resolving the situation in any of these ways will be rewarded with XP.
- If the player kills the Ripperdoc, the hospital will be closed for a few days and finally a new Ripperdoc will take over.
  - Requires freeing the old Ripperdoc
- If the player makes the Ripperdoc pay, the Ripperdoc will be angry but compliant.
- If the player goes after the client, the client will
  - be dead and not appear in the game anymore
  - be scared and pay the player more
  - be angry and call goons on the player (only if player is combative, so the client expected to need support)
  - be impressed and offer the player a better job
  - be intimidated and leave the Doc and Player alone
- If the player pays out of his own pocket, the job will be done.

The only way to get no XP is to ignore the job completely.
- If the player ignores the job, the client will hire someone else and the player will not get any reward. The ripperdoc will be attacked in a scripted event.

These options offer a solution at least for the basic three player types:
 - Fighter
 - Diplomat
 - Thief

You can be the good guy, the bad guy, or the guy who doesn't care.

There is one clever and cool way to solve the quest, and that is to steal the money from the client
and give it right back to him.

The Relationship Between Daniel Harker and Drake Gallows
Dr. Daniel Harker – The Practical Idealist
Daniel Harker is a brilliant and pragmatic ripperdoc, fiercely dedicated to advancing human evolution through technology. His partnership with Eschaton Biotech is born not out of greed, but a belief in the necessity of large-scale, corporate-backed solutions to solve humanity's biggest problems, including the environmental collapse and societal degradation. Harker believes that the corporations, with their resources and influence, are the only entities capable of advancing technological solutions fast enough to save humanity.

Harker has no illusions about the corporations' ethics but believes that working within the system is the only realistic path to progress. He is willing to make morally questionable decisions, like experimenting with cutting-edge cyberware on unwilling subjects or sacrificing the lives of a few for the greater good. In his mind, these sacrifices are necessary for the survival of the human race.

Dr. Drake Gallows – The Reluctant Rebel
Drake Gallows, on the other hand, is driven by a deep resentment toward the corporations. He once believed in the same things as Harker but witnessed firsthand the human cost of corporate control—people becoming test subjects, lives lost in the name of "advancement." He turned away from the corporate world and began offering medical help to those the corporations had discarded, the poor and the marginalized.

Gallows, however, is not a pure-hearted idealist. His break from the corporations was partly motivated by personal tragedy: Eschaton Biotech had once used his own family as subjects in one of their cyberware tests, leading to their deaths. His vendetta against the corporations is personal, and it drives him to dangerous extremes. In his hatred for the system, Gallows began working with insurgent groups, including a radical underground faction that uses extreme measures to oppose corporate dominance.

This has led Gallows to take increasingly desperate actions, including developing illegal and unstable cyberware that could have catastrophic consequences. While his heart is with the people, his judgment has become clouded by vengeance, and his methods grow more reckless by the day. He is willing to unleash tech that could harm or even kill the people he claims to protect if it means striking a blow against the corporations.

Why One Doctor Imprisoned the Other
Drake Gallows was imprisoned by Daniel Harker for a combination of reasons, but the core issue was that Gallows’ actions were becoming too dangerous. Harker discovered that Gallows had been working with a radical anti-corporate group to develop a highly unstable piece of cyberware—a neural implant that, if unleashed, could cause widespread neurological damage to users. Gallows viewed it as a necessary risk to create a weapon against corporate control. Harker, horrified at the potential fallout, decided that Gallows had crossed a line.

Harker's Motive: While Harker still believes in progress through corporate partnership, he also genuinely wants to protect the people of Edge Haven. In his mind, Gallows' reckless actions threaten to unleash chaos that would harm far more than just the corporations—innocent people would die or be maimed by the unstable technology Gallows is working on. By imprisoning Gallows, Harker sees himself as preventing a disaster. He views it as a tough but necessary decision to protect the greater good.

Gallows' Motive: Gallows, on the other hand, sees Harker’s imprisonment of him as the ultimate betrayal—not just of their personal relationship but of their shared belief that technology should serve humanity. Gallows believes that corporate control is the real threat, and anything that disrupts that control, even if dangerous, is worth pursuing. In his view, Harker has sold out to the corporations and abandoned the ideals they once shared. Gallows argues that the only way to break free from corporate dominance is to take extreme action, and Harker’s reluctance to do so makes him complicit in the system's oppression.

The Dilemma
The player, upon discovering Gallows' imprisonment, is faced with a hard choice. Each doctor has valid points, and neither is clearly "right" or "wrong." The corporations are indeed corrupt and exploitative, but they are also the only entities with the power to develop large-scale technological solutions that could save humanity from total collapse.

If the player sides with Daniel Harker: They choose stability and order, recognizing that while the corporations are flawed, working within the system is the only way to prevent mass chaos and destruction. Harker's approach—while cold and utilitarian—protects the larger population from the potentially devastating effects of Gallows' radical technologies. However, siding with Harker means allowing the corporations to continue their stranglehold on society, and the player might feel complicit in perpetuating the very oppression they set out to fight.

If the player sides with Drake Gallows: They opt to strike at the heart of corporate control, believing that true change can only come from outside the system. Freeing Gallows and assisting in his plans could destabilize the corporations' power over Edge Haven and potentially inspire a broader rebellion. However, Gallows' methods are dangerous, and there's no guarantee that the unstable cyberware he’s developing won’t cause widespread harm. The player risks unleashing a wave of destruction that could harm the very people they want to protect.

The Underlying Theme: Compromise vs. Revolution
This conflict reflects the broader theme of compromise vs. revolution that runs through your game world. The dilemma forces the player to question their own values—whether they believe that slow, incremental change within a broken system is better than radical action that could topple the system but at great cost.

Harker represents compromise: The belief that sometimes working with a corrupt system is necessary to achieve the greater good.
Gallows represents revolution: The belief that drastic, dangerous action is needed to break free from oppression, even if the consequences are severe.
The player’s decision will reflect how they view the world—do they believe in calculated pragmatism, or do they believe that real change requires a leap into the unknown, no matter the risk?
